About Victor

"The highest calling of a social scientist is not to describe the world as it is, but to help build the world as it should be."

Victor Kilanko, PhD is the Founder of Pan-African Innovation City, a campus rising in Nigeria, built to develop the people, ideas, and enterprises that build Africa.

He founded Pan-African Innovation City around a single conviction: Africa does not lack talent, ambition, or ideas. It lacks the institutions that turn talent into mastery, ideas into enterprises, and enterprises into shared prosperity. Pan-African Innovation City is his answer, a place where Africans master world class skills and apply them to the industries, farms, and communities the continent's future depends on. Its first two centers, the Skill Center and the Agricultural Center, open in Phase 1 in 2028.

His work runs along three commitments that reinforce one another: economic development, human development, and community development. He believes a society is only as strong as the opportunity available to its people, and that opportunity can be built deliberately, with evidence and patience.

Before founding Pan-African Innovation City, Victor spent his career as an economist and causal inference scientist. He earned a PhD in Economics from Claremont Graduate University, trained across two continents, and served as an economist at the Cicero Institute, where his research used the full frontier of quasi experimental design to measure what public policies actually do. He built catviz, a causal inference visualization framework and freely available R package on CRAN, and has reviewed for the Journal of Policy Analysis and Management. That discipline, knowing what works and why, is the engine he now points at Africa.

His conviction is simple: Africa's future will be built by Africans with the knowledge, skills, and opportunity to create. He has decided to spend his life building the place where that work begins.

My Message

Building the Africa That Should Be

I believe Africa's future will be built by Africans. Not by aid, not by extraction, but by African talent equipped with world class skills and pointed at African problems. My work is grounded in measurement, not opinion. In causal proof, not ideology.

A single conviction animates everything I do: a continent is only as strong as the opportunity available to its people. This is not merely a moral statement. It is a testable, measurable, economic fact.

Africa is the youngest, fastest growing region on earth, with the largest pool of untapped human potential in the world. What it lacks is not talent but the institutions that turn talent into mastery, ideas into enterprises, and enterprises into shared prosperity.

That is the gap I have decided to spend my life closing. I build the people, ideas, and enterprises that make Africa more productive and more prosperous, one city at a time.

6 in 10
Africans are under age 25

The world's youngest continent

About six in every ten Africans are younger than 25. No continent has more young people ready to learn, work, and build. The talent is already here. What is missing is the training, tools, and opportunity to put it to work.

UN DESA, World Population Prospects.

10 to 12M
Young people join the workforce every year

Jobs for a rising generation

Every year, 10 to 12 million young Africans start looking for work, but the formal economy creates only about 3 million jobs for them. The gap is not a shortage of talent. It is a shortage of the skills, enterprises, and industries that turn talent into good work.

African Development Bank, Jobs for Youth in Africa.

60%
Of the world's unused farmland is in Africa

From food importer to food producer

Africa holds roughly 60% of the planet's uncultivated farmland, yet still spends billions importing food it could grow at home. With modern skills, research, and agribusiness, the continent can feed itself and raise the value of everything it grows.

African Development Bank; FAO.

Under 2%
Of the world's manufacturing happens in Africa

Value built at home

Africa makes less than 2% of the world's manufactured goods. It sells raw materials cheaply and buys back finished products at a premium. Building the enterprises and industries that add value at home is how growth becomes lasting prosperity.

UNIDO, International Yearbook of Industrial Statistics.

What if Africa's greatest resource, its people, were fully equipped and fully deployed? That is not charity. It is the highest return investment on earth. I build the people, ideas, and enterprises that make it real, one city at a time.

My Story

The Chapter No Journal Would Publish

"No cure. No hope. The prognosis was death. Whatever years remained would be spent entirely on chemotherapy."

That was the diagnosis. Stage-four terminal cancer, spread to his back and leg. His left leg had folded and would not straighten. Victor could not walk, eat, or function normally. The doctors gave him four to six years, years he would spend on chemotherapy, not living.

During radiation and chemotherapy, he began studying the Bible on healing, and came to understand something that changed everything. He claimed those promises. He repeated them. Slowly, his health began to improve. His leg straightened. The pain subsided.

Today there is no cancer. There is no chemotherapy. There is only the work, and the deep conviction that every day since is a gift that demands to be spent on something that matters.
